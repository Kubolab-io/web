How to get found when people ask an AI who to hire

Written by

Kubo team

Date

November 4, 2026

More people now ask ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini who to hire, instead of scrolling a job board. The model answers with a few names. If yours is not there, that company never saw you.

These tools do not guess. They repeat what they can read. A vague page, a profile with no role and no city, or a CV that is only an image is easy to skip.

Two ways this shows up

1. You use AI to write. It can draft a role description or a profile summary. A person should still read it before it goes live.

2. AI is the front door. Someone asks who they should hire for a role in Colombia, and the answer is built from public pages. Clear text is what gets a name into that answer.

Say the job in one sentence

State the role, the skills, and where the person works. “Senior accountant in Bogotá, full time, remote for a US company” is something a model can repeat. A pile of buzzwords is not.

Put it in text, not only inside an image. A salary range or a service list hidden in a banner is often invisible to these tools.

A five-minute check

Ask ChatGPT and Claude the question a buyer would ask: who can I hire in Colombia for this role, and what do you know about this person or this company?

If you do not appear, or the answer is wrong, the public page needs a clearer sentence. Nobody can promise a model will recommend you. A specific page makes it more likely, and it is easier for a person to read too.

On Kubo, that sentence is the profile: skills, English level, and the salary range, in text, before anyone gets on a call.

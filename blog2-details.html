Freelancers, nearshoring, or full-time hires in Latin America

Written by

Juan Rincón, commercial director

Date

December 14, 2024

Demand for skilled people keeps growing, and more companies look at Latin America as a way to build a team at a lower cost. The real choice is freelancers, a nearshore team, or full-time employees. Each one fits a different job.

This guide walks through the tradeoffs so you can pick the one that matches the work.

Hiring freelancers

Freelancers are flexible and cheaper for a defined project. You skip benefits, local taxes, and an office. People across Latin America are strong in software, design, and marketing, and they can often start quickly. The downside is split attention: many of them serve several clients at once, you have less control over the work, and data security is on you. Great for a short project. A weak fit when you want someone for the long run.

What nearshoring is good for

Nearshoring means handing the work to a team or agency in a nearby country, such as one in Latin America. You get more structure than a pile of separate freelancers, without employing each person yourself.

It usually costs more than freelancers and much less than a US team, with a dedicated group that works only on your projects.

The provider often handles contracts, payroll, and taxes, so the admin does not land on you.

The tradeoff is control. A third-party team is one step removed from you, onboarding can take longer than a freelancer, and it is the wrong tool for a two-week task.

Full-time employees in Latin America

A full-time employee is the right shape when you want someone for the long run: steadier, closer to how your company works, and easier to direct.

They work for one company, so the attention is not split. A stable team also makes communication simpler than rotating contractors.

The work stays on your goals instead of jumping between clients. Over time that is also how a remote group starts to feel like part of the company.

The cost is fixed. You pay a salary and local benefits, and you have to follow local labor law. Hiring takes longer, and shrinking the team later is harder than ending a freelance contract.

Which one fits

If you need a short burst of a specific skill, use a freelancer. If you want a dedicated team and someone else to hold the legal relationship, nearshoring fits. If you want a person on your team for the long run, hire them full time.
